# Bias-Robust Bayesian Meta-Analysis Pipeline

A reproducible R workflow for **bias-robust Bayesian reanalysis of meta-analytic datasets** using Robust Bayesian Meta-Analysis with Publication-Selection Model Averaging (**RoBMA-PSMA**). The pipeline takes study-level effect sizes and standard errors as input and returns model-averaged posterior summaries, inclusion Bayes factors for the effect / heterogeneity / bias-adjustment components, diagnostic z-plots, and standardized cross-study visualizations.

The repository accompanies a methods-focused evidential audit applied to **published nutrition intervention meta-analyses** (175 outcomes across 8 domains). The nutrition corpus is the worked example; the workflow itself is intended for any literature whose primary studies can be reduced to standardized effect sizes and standard errors.

---

## Methodological Contribution

Conventional meta-analytic summaries report pooled estimates, confidence intervals, and *p*-values. These describe an estimator under a chosen model, but they do not directly quantify how strongly the data support an effect, support its absence, or support the need for bias adjustment. They also rarely incorporate publication selection, small-study effects, and model uncertainty into the same inferential object.

This pipeline implements a different default. For each dataset it fits two models on identical inputs:

1. an **unadjusted Bayesian random-effects baseline**, and
2. **RoBMA-PSMA**, which jointly averages over the presence/absence of an effect, the presence/absence of heterogeneity, and a family of bias-adjustment mechanisms (selection models and PET/PEESE-style small-study-effect adjustments).

Both fits are produced through the same computational path, so the contrast between them isolates the impact of bias-adjustment and model uncertainty rather than confounding it with implementation differences across published syntheses. Inclusion Bayes factors for the effect, heterogeneity, and bias components are reported alongside model-averaged point estimates and credible intervals, allowing evidence for absence and evidence for adjustment to be quantified directly rather than inferred from a non-significant *p*-value.

**Primary contribution.** The repository's primary contribution is the **reusable evidential-audit workflow itself** — a documented, end-to-end pipeline that turns the standard meta-analytic data structure (study-level *g* and *SE_g*) into bias-adjusted posterior summaries, inclusion Bayes factors, diagnostics, and cross-study visualizations under a single configuration. The nutrition application is included as a fully worked demonstration; the same scripts apply unchanged to other intervention literatures.

## Worked Example: Nutrition Intervention Meta-Analyses

The included application reanalyzes a purposive corpus of published nutrition intervention meta-analyses spanning eight domains: **caffeine, creatine, diet, fasting, fiber, omega-3, protein, and vitamin D** (175 outcomes total). For each intervention–outcome pairing, study-level effect sizes were extracted from the published synthesis, standardized to Hedges' *g* with associated standard errors, and refit under the unified pipeline. A central design principle was to **preserve the published meta-analytic constructions as much as possible**, so the full reconstructed study set is the analysis of record by default. `_excl` variants were created only when the full reconstructed dataset produced a clearly pathological RoBMA-PSMA fit — most commonly when one or a few extreme positive outliers dominated the bias-adjusted ensemble; in those cases studies were removed sequentially from the largest positive outlier until the first stable, interpretable fit was reached. The exclusion procedure is intentionally minimal and conservative, and the full set is always preserved in the paired extraction workbook. See [`data_dictionary.md`](data_dictionary.md) for the full specification.

The substantive findings of that audit are reported in the accompanying manuscript. From a repository standpoint, the nutrition corpus serves as a fully worked example: cleaned analysis-ready CSVs, derived summary tables, and the figures that appear in the paper are all generated by running the pipeline as documented below.

Conventions:

- Each dataset has a stable **stem** of the form `<author><year>_<topic>_<outcome>` (e.g., `morton2018_protein_muscle_strength`). The stem is preserved through every artifact the pipeline produces.
- `_excl` variants append `_excl` to the stem and are analyzed as distinct datasets. They exist only when the full reconstructed study set produced a pathological RoBMA-PSMA fit and a minimally modified set was needed to reach a stable, interpretable bias-robust fit; see [`data_dictionary.md`](data_dictionary.md).
- Raw extraction workbooks (`.xlsx`) are kept beside the analysis-ready CSV they derive from so that every analyzed value, including any rows held out of an `_excl` fit, is traceable.

### What is included in the public repository

The public repository contains the **data, scripts, summary tables, and figures** required to understand and reproduce the workflow end-to-end. Per-dataset fitted RoBMA objects (`fit_RoBMA_*.rds`, `fit_RE_*.rds`, `zcurve_*.rds`) are large and **may be omitted** from the public archive: they can be regenerated from the analysis-ready CSVs and the `CONFIG` block in `20_robma_fit.R` (see Quick Start), up to small numerical variation across `RoBMA` and JAGS releases. All published figures are generated from the per-topic `*_robma_summary.csv` files, which are included.

## Quick Start

**Requirements.** R (≥ 4.2 recommended) and the [`RoBMA`](https://cran.r-project.org/package=RoBMA) package, which itself requires a working JAGS installation. Plotting and aggregation rely on `tidyverse`, `patchwork`, `fs`, and `readr`. A pinned environment specification (`renv.lock`) is provided alongside the public release so that the exact package versions used to produce the manuscript figures can be restored.

**Reproducing a single dataset.**

```r
source("scripts/10_load_data.R")
source("scripts/20_robma_fit.R")

load_datasets(topic = "fiber", author = "jovanovski2019")
fit_robma_models(jovanovski2019_fiber_hba1c,
                 dataset_name = "jovanovski2019_fiber_hba1c")
```

**Reproducing an entire topic (with resume support).**

```r
source("scripts/10_load_data.R")
source("scripts/20_robma_fit.R")
source("scripts/40_batch_fit.R")

batch_fit(topic = "fiber", resume = TRUE)
source("scripts/50_topic_analysis.R"); topic_analysis(topic = "fiber")
```

**Regenerating the cross-topic overview.**

```r
source("scripts/60_overview.R")
```

`resume = TRUE` skips datasets whose fitted artifacts already exist on disk, so re-running the pipeline after a partial run is safe and inexpensive. With no fitted objects present, a full rebuild is launched automatically.

## Pipeline Overview

The six scripts execute in numbered order and form a discover → fit → diagnose → batch → summarize → aggregate workflow.

**`10_load_data.R` — Discovery and loading.** Scans the `data/<topic>/<author>/` hierarchy, catalogs available CSVs (`list_datasets()`), and loads selected files into the calling environment (`load_datasets()`). Filters meta/helper files automatically.

**`20_robma_fit.R` — Core model fitting.** Given a data frame with `g` and `se_g`, fits an unadjusted Bayesian random-effects model and a RoBMA-PSMA ensemble using the spike-and-slab sampler. Persists fitted models and z-curve objects as `.rds` files under `output/<topic>/<author>/` and appends a row to the per-topic summary CSV containing posterior estimates, credible intervals, and inclusion Bayes factors. MCMC settings (chains, iterations, seed, effect direction) are centralized in the `CONFIG` list at the top of the script.

**`30_robma_analysis.R` — Per-fit diagnostics.** Generates console summaries and z-distribution plots for an individual fit, including a posterior-predictive z-plot and a pre-publication-bias extrapolation panel. Used interactively after fitting to inspect a specific outcome.

**`40_batch_fit.R` — Batch orchestration.** Runs `20_robma_fit.R` over all datasets matching a `topic` / `author` / `file` filter. Supports dry-run planning, resume mode (skips datasets whose artifacts already exist), and optional parallel execution. Returns a status table with per-dataset outcomes, timing, and error messages.

**`50_topic_analysis.R` — Topic-level synthesis.** Reads `output/<topic>/<topic>_robma_summary.csv` and produces a paired baseline-vs-RoBMA boxplot, a forest plot with dual credible intervals, and a three-panel orchard plot of log₁₀ Bayes factors for the effect, heterogeneity, and bias components. Computes derived metrics (shrinkage, sign flips, evidence-threshold counts) and appends a per-topic row to the consolidated `output/topic_summary.csv`.

**`60_overview.R` — Cross-topic aggregation.** Auto-discovers every `*_robma_summary.csv` under `output/`, merges them into a unified outcome-level table, and produces faceted orchard plots for the effect / heterogeneity / bias components, topic-level violin plots and a manuscript-ready combined violin stack, two cross-topic shrinkage boxplots, and a joint effect-vs-bias-adjustment evidence scatter. Writes an evidence-bin summary (`output/overview/topic_summary.csv`) with one row per topic plus an aggregated "Overall" row, and a supplement-ready intervention–outcome dataset listing.

A more detailed per-script manual (inputs, outputs, dependencies, assumptions) is preserved in [`documentation/scripts_readme.md`](documentation/scripts_readme.md).

## Input Data Structure

The pipeline treats every dataset as a single intervention–outcome pairing reduced to a minimal effect-size representation. Each analysis-ready CSV must contain:

| Column | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| `g` | Standardized effect size (Hedges' *g*; Cohen's *d* is acceptable when small-sample correction is not recoverable). |
| `se_g` | Standard error of the corresponding effect size. |

Optional columns (e.g., a `study` label) are tolerated and pass through to the summary tables. A minimum of three studies is required for fitting.

**Effect-direction convention.** Effects are sign-aligned so that a positive value reflects the direction favored by the intervention hypothesis. RoBMA-PSMA is then fit with `effect_direction = "positive"`, which orients the one-sided selection components but does not constrain the sign of the fitted pooled effect.

**Naming convention.** Files use the stem `<author><year>_<topic>_<outcome>.csv` (e.g., `morton2018_protein_muscle_strength.csv`). When the full reconstructed dataset produced a clearly pathological RoBMA-PSMA fit, an `_excl` companion CSV (e.g., `nunes2022_protein_lean_body_mass_excl.csv`) holds the minimally modified study set actually used for the bias-robust fit; the full reconstructed set is always retained in the paired extraction workbook (`.xlsx`). `_excl` variants are analyzed by the pipeline as distinct datasets. See [`data_dictionary.md`](data_dictionary.md) for the complete `_excl` policy.

## Outputs

**Per dataset.**

- `fit_RE_<stem>.rds` and `fit_RoBMA_<stem>.rds` — fitted model objects. **May be omitted from the public repository** (large; regenerable from the analysis-ready CSV via `40_batch_fit.R`).
- `zcurve_RE_<stem>.rds` and `zcurve_RoBMA_<stem>.rds` — z-curve representations used in diagnostics. **May be omitted from the public repository** on the same grounds.
- A row appended to `output/<topic>/<topic>_robma_summary.csv` containing posterior summaries, credible intervals, and inclusion Bayes factors for the effect, heterogeneity, and bias components. **Always committed** — every figure in the workflow is regenerable from this CSV.

**Per topic.** Saved under `output/<topic>/plots/`:

- `<topic>_bias_correction_comparison.pdf` — paired boxplot of baseline RE vs. RoBMA-PSMA estimates.
- `<topic>_forest_plot_comparison.pdf` — forest plot with dual credible intervals.
- `<topic>_orchard_combined.pdf` — three-panel orchard plot (log₁₀ BF for effect, heterogeneity, bias).

In addition, `50_topic_analysis.R` appends a topic-level row to `output/topic_summary.csv` with shrinkage, sign-flip, and evidence-threshold counts.

**Cross-topic.** Saved under `output/overview/`:

- `topic_summary.csv` — evidence-bin counts and proportions on log₁₀ BF axes for the effect, heterogeneity, and bias components, with one row per topic plus an aggregated "Overall" row.
- `intervention_outcome_datasets.csv` and `intervention_outcome_datasets_table.tex` — supplement-ready listing of every analyzed intervention–outcome dataset.
- `orchard_effect.pdf`, `orchard_heterogeneity.pdf`, `orchard_bias.pdf` — outcome-level orchard plots faceted by topic.
- `violin_effect.pdf`, `violin_heterogeneity.pdf`, `violin_bias.pdf` — topic-level distribution of evidence for each component.
- `violin_stack_fullpage.pdf` (with a `.png` companion) — manuscript-ready combined three-panel evidence figure.
- `boxplot_combined_horizontal.pdf` and `boxplot_strip_single.pdf` — cross-topic shrinkage from baseline RE to RoBMA-PSMA.
- `scatter_effect_vs_bias.pdf` — joint effect-vs-bias-adjustment evidence scatter, with the "bias without robust effect" region shaded.

Note that two files share the name `topic_summary.csv` in different roles: `output/topic_summary.csv` is the per-topic accumulator written by `50_topic_analysis.R` (one row per topic, focused on shrinkage and evidence counts), while `output/overview/topic_summary.csv` is the cross-topic evidence-bin summary written by `60_overview.R` (one row per topic + "Overall", focused on log₁₀ BF threshold counts and proportions).

Because every figure is built from a summary CSV rather than from session state, plots can be regenerated without rerunning the underlying MCMC.

## Adapting the Workflow to a New Domain

The pipeline is not specific to nutrition. To apply it to another literature (exercise science, education interventions, behavioral trials, etc.):

1. **Standardize each meta-analysis to the input contract.** Produce one CSV per intervention–outcome pairing with columns `g` and `se_g`. Sign-align effects so positive corresponds to the favored direction for that outcome.
2. **Place CSVs in `data/<your-topic>/<author><year>/`.** Keep the paired extraction workbook alongside the CSV for traceability.
3. **Run the pipeline.** Source `10_load_data.R` and `20_robma_fit.R`, then call `batch_fit(topic = "<your-topic>")`. Topic-level and overview scripts will pick up the new topic automatically; the cross-topic overview discovers any directory containing a `*_robma_summary.csv`.
4. **(Optional) Adjust MCMC settings.** Defaults in `CONFIG` (6 chains, 100k post-burnin samples, fixed seed) are appropriate for typical meta-analyses. Larger or harder-to-fit datasets can use the same expanded settings the nutrition audit used for outcomes that did not converge under default settings.

No script edits are required to add a new topic. Domain-specific labels, axis ranges, or plot titles in `50_topic_analysis.R` and `60_overview.R` may be customized as desired.

## Reproducibility and Repository Notes

- **Reproducibility.** Given the analysis-ready CSVs and the `CONFIG` block in `20_robma_fit.R` (including the fixed seed), the reported posterior summaries and inclusion Bayes factors should be reproducible up to small numerical variation across `RoBMA` and JAGS releases. The `renv.lock` shipped with the public release pins the exact package versions used for the manuscript figures. Z-plot histogram appearance and minor formatting may vary across plotting-package versions.
- **Resume safety.** `40_batch_fit.R` skips datasets whose `.rds` artifacts already exist, so partial runs and incremental additions are safe.
- **Immutable raw inputs.** Extraction workbooks and analysis-ready CSVs are never overwritten by the pipeline; all derived products live under `output/`.
- **Regenerable artifacts.** Large fitted `.rds` model objects (`fit_RE_*.rds`, `fit_RoBMA_*.rds`, `zcurve_*.rds`) **may be omitted from the public repository** when the included data, configuration, and scripts are sufficient to reproduce the reported summaries and figures. The committed per-topic `*_robma_summary.csv` files carry every quantity needed for the per-topic and cross-topic figure layers, so plots can be regenerated without rerunning MCMC. Expect a full nutrition rebuild of the fits themselves to take several hours of compute on a multi-core workstation; individual topics complete much faster.
- **Software environment.** The pipeline has been run with `RoBMA` (≥ 3.0) under R (≥ 4.2) with a working JAGS installation. A pinned `renv.lock` is committed alongside the public release so that the exact package versions used to generate the manuscript figures can be restored with `renv::restore()`.
